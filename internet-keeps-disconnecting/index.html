Internet keeps disconnecting randomly? Record what’s happening.

Random disconnects are difficult to troubleshoot because the connection may be normal again by the time you investigate.

First, narrow down what you can

  1. Check whether the router or modem shows an obvious fault.
  2. Check whether another device loses connectivity at the same time.
  3. Note whether Windows stays connected to Wi‑Fi or Ethernet while internet access disappears.
  4. Write down the exact start time, recovery time, and approximate duration.
  5. Keep the same notes across multiple incidents so a pattern can emerge.

Why timestamps matter

“The internet dropped again” describes the symptom, but it is difficult to compare with router logs or support records. A timestamped entry gives you a concrete incident to investigate.

9:42:17 PM — connectivity lost
9:43:00 PM — connectivity recovered
Duration — 43 seconds
Other device affected — unknown

A simple manual outage log

You can start with a text file or spreadsheet. Record the date, start time, end time, duration, whether your local gateway was reachable, whether other devices were affected, and anything you changed before recovery.

Important: a disconnect alone does not tell you who caused it. Local Wi‑Fi, the router, DNS, an upstream provider, or another part of the path may be involved.

When manual logging becomes the problem

Intermittent failures rarely happen when you are ready with a stopwatch. If the issue keeps returning, continuous timestamped observations are more useful than trying to reconstruct the event from memory. That is the recording step Internet Black Box is designed to automate.
