How to document intermittent internet problems for your ISP

When a connection recovers before support can inspect it, a consistent incident record can make the conversation much more concrete.

What makes an incident record useful?

  1. Exact timestamp: when the problem started and recovered.
  2. Duration: seconds or minutes affected.
  3. Local path: whether the PC could still reach its local gateway.
  4. External connectivity: whether checks beyond the local network succeeded.
  5. Recovery: what observations returned to normal and when.
  6. Repeatability: the same fields across multiple incidents.

Separate observation from blame

Good evidence says what the computer observed. It should not jump from “internet access failed” to “the ISP caused it” without supporting information. A local gateway failure, for example, points to a different troubleshooting path than a reachable gateway with repeated external failures.

What to share with support

Send the smallest useful package: the incident timestamps, duration, relevant connectivity observations, and the report or log that preserves them. Keep your description factual: what happened, when it happened, how long it lasted, and what was observable at the time.
